Contacts, correspondence and the contact graph

People menu · Castellum

1. Contacts

Open People > Contacts (⇧⌘C, or g then c). Castellum builds the list from your synced mail, so a new profile shows “No contacts yet — sync a mailbox to populate this list.”

Use the People tab to filter by name or email. Select a contact to see how many messages you received from and sent to them, when you first and last saw them, and a This is you mark on your own addresses. You can set a display name that overrides the one in the mail headers, add notes, and tick Hidden to keep someone out of the default list (show them again with the Hidden toggle above the list). Click Save to keep your edits.

2. Adding information by hand

In a contact's Enrichment section, click Add info…, paste text you already have about the person (a profile you copied, for instance), click Preview to see which fields Castellum found, then Save. Castellum only reads the text you paste; it does not look anything up online.

3. Correspondence with a sender

Select a message and choose View > Show Correspondence with Sender. The message list switches to all mail to and from that person, with a bar above it showing how many messages, sent and received, first and last dates, and a 12-month activity strip. Close the bar to go back.

4. The graph

The Graph tab in the Contacts window draws your contacts as a network, placed by a force-directed layout. You can filter it to one project.

5. Address suggestions when writing

When you type in To, Cc or Bcc, Castellum suggests people from your mail. If you allow it, it also uses your macOS Contacts. Castellum asks for that permission once, the first time you type an address, and works without it.
